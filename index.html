My name is Dylan Tse, and I'm a Global Health major at the University of California, Riverside. I also play Division I women's basketball. I grew up in South Pasadena, California, and planned to go to UCR to further my education and my basketball career. Which, I have had many experiences that have taught me lessons. I learned to be vocal, social, perserverence, and confident. Most of my professional experiences have come from what I learned from basketball, but I had a job at a boba place that taught me the importance of connecting with customers. I also used to volunteer at a hospital, which teaches me to be social and more extroverted. This has taught me time management skills and being organized. I have also learned from all of my experiences to say composed under pressure and learn how to deal with stressful environemnts.

Beyond my work experiences and volunteer work, have also coached younger athletes and helped organize basketball clinics for children of various ages and skill levels. These experiences strengthened my communication and leadership skills while teaching me the importance of adapting to each person’s individual learning style. I believe this ability will be valuable in any career, and I find it especially rewarding to see others improve their skills and gain confidence over time. My interest in Global Health came from me playing basketball and volunteering at a hospital. I have always wanted to help people, especially athletes. As a athlete myself, I have always wanted to help injured players get back to the sport they love and continue building their confidence. Since injured ahtletes might be down because of their injuries, I want to give them a hope that they are able to get back or even better from where they left off.

Being an athlete has shaped the way I understand teamwork and collaboration. Basketball brings together individuals with different personalities, backgrounds, and strengths who must work together toward a common goal. I see many similarities between a successful basketball team and a successful business, as both depend on effective leadership, clear communication, mutual trust, accountability, and a supportive environment to succeed. In the future, I hope that I can encourage younger athletes and be a role model for them. My interest in helping grow confidence and heal most people is what keeps me motivated. Since I enjoy sports and giving back to the community, this is what I believe will be enjoyable. Overall, my background as a student-athlete, trainer, helper, and global health student has shaped who I am today. I want to contnue to build my professional experiences and build my career in my future.
